Four things

It does four things, carefully.

No timeline, no albums to manage, no AI search. Just getting files safely up — and back down when you ask.

  1. Upload

    Drop files or folders. See counts, sizes and what will be skipped before a single byte leaves your disk.

  2. Backup

    Point it at a folder. New and changed files go up, immediately or on an interval. Subfolders are mirrored.

  3. Activity

    A persistent queue you can pause, resume, cancel and retry. It survives quitting, crashes and sleep.

  4. Restore

    Bring originals back by history, folder or album. Every file is checked by size and MD5 before it lands.

One-way backup

Water only flows one way.

New photos travel up. Deleting something on your computer never deletes it from the cloud. Try it.

      Transfer queue

      Interrupted? It checks before it re-sends.

      If the app quits mid-upload, nothing is blindly sent twice. On the next launch Rainforest compares what reached the cloud by name and MD5, confirms it, and retries only what didn’t make it.

        Small things

        Made of small decisions.

        • External drives are respected

          Unplug a backup source and the rule pauses as “source unavailable” instead of treating your library as deleted.

        • Half-written files wait

          A file still being copied or exported is left alone until its size settles.

        • Folders keep their shape

          Local subfolders are mirrored under the destination. Albums stay flat, as they should.

        • Your session stays local

          Sign in through Amazon’s own window. Cookies are stored in a permission-locked local vault and redacted from every log.

        • English and 简体中文

          Follows your system language, or pick one from Settings or the View menu.

        • Native on every desktop

          macOS, Windows and Linux on Intel and ARM. A single Go binary, no Electron.
